Being easy to work with on an async team is not a personality trait. It is a set of behaviors, most of which can be learned in an afternoon and refined over a few weeks of practice.

## Write like your message will be read while you are asleep

Because it will be. On a distributed team, no one is waiting for the follow-up clarification you planned to send if they had questions. The first message has to carry all the weight.

This means front-loading your ask. Put the actual request or decision needed in the first two sentences, not the last. Add context after, not before. If you need someone to review a document, say that immediately, then explain why, then give the deadline. People who bury the action item under three paragraphs of background force everyone else to read everything before knowing whether the message is even relevant to them.

It also means including your reasoning, briefly. "Can you check this?" is a worse message than "Can you check this before Thursday? The client reviews it Friday and I want another set of eyes on the pricing section specifically." The second version lets someone help you accurately without a back-and-forth round trip.

## Respect the latency

Async work has built-in delay, and genuinely easy collaborators accept this instead of routing around it. If you send a message and then follow up an hour later asking if they saw it, you have created a synchronous dependency inside a supposedly async system. That is friction for the other person.

The discipline here is planning your own work so that you are not routinely in a position where you need an answer in two hours. When you are chronically in that position, the problem is usually upstream — a planning or scoping issue, not a communication issue. Easy collaborators do not create artificial urgency by waiting too long to surface a blocker.

When something actually is urgent, say so plainly and say why. "This is blocking me from finishing the deployment today" is a legitimate escalation. "Hey, quick question" at 4pm on a Friday, with no indication of urgency, is not.

## Keep a short, findable record of what you decided

Async teams lose an enormous amount of time relitigating decisions that were made in a Slack thread six weeks ago and are now impossible to find. Easy collaborators close the loop in writing, somewhere durable.

This does not require elaborate documentation. It can be as simple as: after a decision gets made in chat, one person posts a two-sentence summary in the relevant project document or ticket. "We decided to use the existing template rather than build a new one. Main reason: timeline." That is it. The habit matters more than the format.

The same principle applies to status. If you finish something, say so somewhere the right people will see it without having to ask you. If you are blocked, surface it in writing the same day you notice it. Radio silence on async teams reads as "everything is fine" whether that is true or not.

## Give feedback that can be acted on in one pass

Vague feedback is expensive on an async team because resolving it requires another round trip. "This doesn't quite feel right" costs at least a day. "The tone in the second paragraph is more formal than the rest of the piece — can you bring it in line with the opening?" costs nothing.

Specific feedback does not require being harsh. It just requires being precise about what you are reacting to and what you would like instead. Easy collaborators do this consistently, and they also distinguish between "this is a problem you need to fix" and "here is a thought for your consideration," so the recipient knows how much weight to give each comment.

## Try this

Take the last substantive message you sent to a teammate — a request, a piece of feedback, a status update, anything. Read it as if you are that person, receiving it at 11pm after a long day, with no access to you for the next eight hours. Ask yourself: do I know exactly what is being asked of me? Do I know when it is needed? Do I have enough context to act without following up? If any answer is no, rewrite that message now. Then apply the same test before you send the next five messages this week.

---

Being easy to work with on an async team is mostly about reducing the cost your work imposes on other people's time and attention. The specifics are learnable. The main requirement is remembering that your teammates are not waiting by their computers for your message.
